# Post-Run Recovery Nutrition

After a hard or long session, early recovery nutrition begins glycogen replacement, muscle repair, and rehydration. The first 30-60 minutes is especially useful when another demanding session follows soon.

## Workshop calculation

| Target | Workshop reference | Example for a 70 kg runner |
|---|---:|---:|
| Carbohydrate | About 1 g/kg | About 70 g |
| Protein | About 0.3 g/kg | About 21 g |
| Carbohydrate-to-protein ratio | About 3:1 or 4:1 | Adjust food portions to the session and next meal |

## When appetite is low

Liquid options can provide energy, protein, and fluid:

- 300-400 ml chocolate soy or dairy milk;
- banana and berry smoothie with soy milk and a protein source;
- oat milk, plant protein powder, and honey shake.

## When the next meal is more than two hours away

Choose a carbohydrate-and-protein snack, such as:

- yogurt or soy yogurt with fruit and cereal;
- toast with a protein topping;
- a filled sandwich.

## When lunch or dinner follows soon

Build a complete meal containing:

- a generous carbohydrate source;
- a meaningful protein source;
- vegetables or fruit;
- a source of healthy fat.

## Safety boundary

These calculations reproduce general workshop guidance, not a mandatory prescription for every runner. Needs vary with body size, session demands, recovery time, health, and overall diet.
