# Runner Fueling Timeline: Before, During, and After Running

Fueling has three connected phases: prepare before the run, maintain energy during longer efforts, and begin recovery afterward.

| Phase | Timing | Main goal | Workshop strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before | About 10 minutes to 2 hours before | Top up available energy without digestive distress | Prioritize carbohydrate; reduce fat and fibre as the run gets closer |
| During | Usually relevant beyond about 75 minutes | Maintain blood glucose and delay fatigue | Use fast-acting carbohydrate; consider sodium according to conditions and individual needs |
| After | First 30-60 minutes after a hard or long run | Begin glycogen replacement and muscle repair | Combine carbohydrate and protein, followed by a complete meal |

## Before the run

Choose familiar foods and adjust the portion to the time available. The closer the start, the smaller and simpler the option should usually become.

## During the run

For runs longer than approximately 75 minutes, the workshop suggests **30-90 g of carbohydrate per hour**. This is a broad range: higher intake is most relevant to long endurance events and requires gradual practice.

## After the run

The workshop uses a carbohydrate-to-protein ratio of approximately **3:1 or 4:1** during early recovery. When appetite is low, a drink or smoothie may be easier than solid food.
