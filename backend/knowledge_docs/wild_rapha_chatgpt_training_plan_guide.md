# Make Your Training Plan with ChatGPT

## Framework

The guide proposes four phases:

| Phase | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Initial | Build consistency, mobility, and an aerobic base |
| Progression | Gradually increase volume and intensity |
| Taper | Reduce training load before race day |
| Recovery | Rest, repair, and reflect after the race |

The source advises gradual progression and states that mileage or intensity should not rise by more than 10% per week. This is a general heuristic, not a universal safety guarantee. Strength, mobility, sleep, nutrition, prior training, and symptoms also affect appropriate load.

## Information requested from the runner

- Goal race and date
- Age, weight, and height
- Sporting history
- Running experience, recent races, and personal bests
- Past injuries and current limitations
- Work and lifestyle
- Weekly training availability
- Terrain preference
- Available equipment
- Historical training data or screenshots

## Prompt workflow

### 1. Establish the coaching context

Ask the AI to act as a running coach and nutrition guide, state the race goal, and provide the runner profile above.

### 2. Share historical training data

Provide readable exports or screenshots and ask the AI to summarize its understanding and identify missing or ambiguous data.

### 3. Conduct an intake interview

Ask for targeted questions before generating a plan. Important topics include current weekly distance, longest recent run, recent consistency, preferred days, pain, medical clearance where relevant, sleep, recovery, dietary preferences, and access to equipment.

### 4. Build a baseline plan

Request a baseline plan emphasizing progressive mileage, balanced recovery, and race-specific preparation.

### 5. Organize the plan into phases

Request the initial, progression, taper, and recovery phases, with weekly distance and the purpose of key sessions.

### 6. Add strength and mobility

The source suggests:

- 2-4 strength sessions per week using push, pull, legs, or plyometric work
- Avoiding leg strength immediately before long runs or interval sessions
- Strength sessions of roughly 30-60 minutes, depending on availability
- Mobility 2-6 times per week for 10-20 minutes, focusing on ankles, hips, and hamstrings

These ranges are broad. The final dose should reflect experience, recovery capacity, and total training load.

### 7. Add nutrition

First collect information on diet, preferences, intolerances, routine, training times, and goals. Then request:

- Guidelines for normal and hard training days
- Pre-run and post-run fueling
- Hydration and electrolytes for longer runs
- A basic race-day fueling strategy
- Practical, mostly whole-food choices

### 8. Export the plan

Ask for a weekly calendar and optionally CSV output for import into a spreadsheet or planning tool.

### 9. Iterate

Example adjustments include:

- Lengthening long runs while keeping total weekly load similar
- Adding a rest day before key sessions
- Simplifying the plan for a beginner
- Adapting road training to trail running

## Safety and retrieval rules

- AI-generated plans do not replace individualized coaching or clinical assessment.
- Do not generate running sessions when the user’s pain-during-running score exceeds the project’s safety threshold of 3/10; request updated feedback or medical clearance instead.
- Plans should be checked for impossible dates, inconsistent weekly totals, abrupt load spikes, and conflicts between hard sessions.
- Nutrition recommendations must respect dietary restrictions and allergies.
