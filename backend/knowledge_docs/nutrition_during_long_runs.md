# During-Run Fueling for Long Runs and Races

For runs beyond roughly 75 minutes, plan carbohydrate intake instead of relying only on water. The goal is to support blood glucose, delay fatigue, and avoid waiting until energy has already crashed.

## Workshop reference range

| Session | General workshop guidance |
|---|---|
| Up to about 75 minutes | During-run carbohydrate may not be necessary for many runners when normal daily fueling is adequate |
| Longer than about 75 minutes | Consider fast-acting carbohydrate during the run |
| Long endurance event | The workshop reference range is 30-90 g carbohydrate per hour, individualized and practised gradually |

## Portable options

- sports drink;
- energy gels;
- chews;
- other portable carbohydrate that the runner has tested.

## Practice protocol

1. Begin with a quantity and product the stomach tolerates.
2. Practise the timing during training, not for the first time on race day.
3. Use water according to product directions and personal tolerance.
4. Increase carbohydrate intake gradually when preparing for longer events.
5. Adapt fluid and sodium planning to heat, duration, sweat losses, and professional advice.

## Important limitation

The 30-90 g/hour range is not one universal dose. The higher end is generally more relevant to prolonged endurance work and may require specific carbohydrate mixtures and gut training.
