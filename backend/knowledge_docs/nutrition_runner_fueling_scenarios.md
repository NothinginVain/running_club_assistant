# Runner Fueling Scenarios and Practical Adjustments

The workshop introduced these cases as discussion prompts. The adjustments below are reasoned applications of its principles, not direct individualized prescriptions.

| Scenario | Observed problem | Practical adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Early runner trains at 06:30 on black coffee and develops dizziness, heavy legs, and pace decline after about 45 minutes | Little available carbohydrate before the session | Test a small fast-carbohydrate option 10-20 minutes before and eat a larger breakfast afterward; persistent dizziness requires medical assessment |
| After-work runner eats lunch at 12:30 and trains at 18:30 without another meal or snack | Six-hour gap before training | Add a carbohydrate-centred snack about 1.5-2 hours before; use a small top-up closer to the run if needed |
| Runner eats oats, chia, flax, berries, and peanut butter 45 minutes before a hard run | High fibre and fat very close to intense exercise | Move the meal earlier or replace it with tested, lower-fibre, lower-fat carbohydrate |
| Runner completes 18 km with water only, then needs days to recover and feels chronically tired | Possible underfueling during the long run and across the day | Practise during-run carbohydrate, use carbohydrate plus protein afterward, and review total daily energy; chronic fatigue warrants professional assessment |

## How to use these examples

Match the principle, not the exact food. Timing, portion, tolerance, health, and session demands differ between runners.
